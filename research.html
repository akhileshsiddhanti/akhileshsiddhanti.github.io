Research & publications

My research asks how cryptographic implementations behave when their assumptions are challenged: what happens when a fault is injected, part of an internal state is exposed, or a hardware response is statistically biased?

I have coauthored work on lightweight stream and block ciphers, physically unclonable functions, and machine learning for fault localization. This work spans research at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, and Nanyang Technological University, Singapore, including my undergraduate thesis on lightweight stream-cipher cryptanalysis.

Research themes

Machine learning for fault localization

Using neural networks to infer where a fault occurred from differences between correct and faulty cipher outputs.

Fault attacks & lightweight cryptography

Evaluating cipher implementations under fault-injection models and studying state and key recovery.

Hardware security & PUFs

Analyzing input-response bias and reliability in arbiter-based physically unclonable functions.
